# Primary Mathematics Grades 1–6 — Complete Renjiao (People's Education Press, 人教版) Topic Map, with WHY for Every Topic

**Date of research:** 6 November 2026 | **Status:** sourced, exhaustive at unit + knowledge-point level; dual-edition (see §0).  
**How to use this report:** §1 gives the two Renjiao orders (old and 2024–26 revised). §2 is the topic-by-topic WHY table — the core of the follow-up instruction: **no procedure without its reason**. §3 resources, §4 licensing/conflicts, §5 abstraction cliffs, §6 the app outline (grade → unit → concept → lesson, with an interaction type for every concept where interaction is possible).

> **The WHY rule applied throughout.** Every row in §2 has four columns: **WHY it works** (visual/intuitive proof), **Why the misconception is wrong**, **Best visualisation**, and **Explain-back** — a sentence the student should be able to say in their own words. If a lesson in the app (§6) teaches only steps, it has failed this rule. The generic WHY proof-patterns are collected in §2.0 so each row can stay short; the row then gives the topic-specific instance.
>
> **A note on "explain it back".** The test is not parroting ("flip the second fraction"). It is: *can the child say why, using the picture?* e.g. "Dividing by 1/2 asks how many halves fit, and there are 2 halves in each whole, so I multiply by 2." Every §2 row ends with that prompt in italics.

## 0. Read this first — two Renjiao editions are in circulation, and they conflict

There is **no single Renjiao chapter order** to report as settled fact in November 2026. Two editions coexist:

| Edition | What it is | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Pre-2024 edition (2012/2013, illustrations redrawn 2022)** | The order most teachers, parents and third-party sites still list. Units in §1A. | Verified live 2026-11-06 on the third-party directory https://www.renjiaoshe.com/xiaoxueshuxue.html (not PEP official; see below). Corroborated by Scribd copies of the 2012 textbooks and by the older summary lists in search results. |
| **B. 2022-standards revised edition, rolling out from autumn 2024** | Re-sequenced to the 2022 standards: multiplication/division are taught together much earlier, some topics move grade, new 综合与实践 (integrated practice) units added, 数学广角 largely absorbed/renamed. Units in §1B. | Documented in https://github.com/xiaoyang111222/cn-math-taxonomy/blob/main/README.md (lines 141–217), which states it checked unit lists against the National Smart Education Platform (basic.smartedu.cn) on 2026-10-22 for G1上, G1下, G2上, G2下, G3上, G3下, G4上, G5上, G5下, G6上 — and that **G4下 and G6下 revised editions had not yet been published** at that check. Sub-section attributions there are explicitly marked 待核 (to be verified). |

**What could NOT be verified against the official PEP site:** PEP's official site is www.pep.com.cn (confirmed: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Education_Press — "Official website www.pep.com.cn"). **No page on pep.com.cn was found that lists all 12 books' units in one place.** The cn-math-taxonomy README states the same finding explicitly ("没有找到人教社（pep.com.cn）一次列全 1–6 年级各册单元的官方目录"). The official e-textbook portal cited in Ministry notices is http://bp.pep.com.cn/jc/ — that URL appears in search-result text (Ministry of Education notice, 14 Feb 2020) but was **not** live-verified in this research; do not treat it as a checked link. **Consequence for the app:** build the content model on *knowledge points with prerequisites* (§2, §6), not on a hard-coded chapter order, and ship a per-edition ordering switch (A vs B). Where §1A and §1B disagree, the report says so; do not silently pick one.

**2022 standards frame (both editions claim alignment to it).** The 2022 《义务教育数学课程标准》 keeps 4 domains but collapses 12 themes into **7 themes for primary**: 数与运算 (Number & Operation), 数量关系 (Quantitative Relationships), 图形的认识与测量 (Shape Recognition & Measurement), 图形的位置与运动 (Position & Movement of Shapes), 数据分类 (Data Classification), 数据的收集、整理与表达 (Data Collection, Organisation & Expression), 随机现象发生的可能性 (Possibility of Random Phenomena). Source: https://www.pep.com.cn/bks/kcjcjf/xszh/202503/W020250410540192903150.pdf and https://github.com/xiaoyang111222/cn-math-taxonomy/blob/main/docs/standards-mapping.md. Stages in the 2022 standard are **1–2, 3–4, 5–6** (not the 2011 split 1–3 / 4–6 — that older split appears in some secondary summaries and is a common source of confusion; the 77 primary standards items are counted 18 + 29 + 30 across those three stages in the standards-mapping file). "常见的量" (common measures: time, money, length, mass) is no longer a standalone theme — it is folded into 综合与实践 and into number/measurement work. Equations are formally moved toward junior high in the 2022 framing, but Renjiao G5 still teaches 用字母表示数 / 简易方程 as a bridge — flag this as a standards-vs-textbook tension, not an error.

**Prerequisite spine (holds in both editions).** Counting & 1-to-1 correspondence → place value (10 = 1 ten) → addition/subtraction without regrouping → regrouping → multiplication as repeated addition/array → division as sharing/grouping (inverse of multiplication) → remainder → multi-digit ×/÷ (distributive law) → fractions as equal sharing (unit fractions) → equivalence (×1) → fraction +/− (same unit) → fraction ×/÷ → decimals as tenths/hundredths (fractions with denominator 10/100) → decimal ×/÷ → ratio as comparison of two quantities → proportion (equal ratios) → percent (ratio to 100) → negative numbers (opposite direction) → area (unit-square counting) → volume (unit-cube counting, V = base area × height). Every §2 row names its prerequisite(s) in the **Needs** column.

## 2. Topic-by-topic: concept, WHY, misconception, visualisation, explain-back

### 2.0 The reusable WHY proofs (teach these once, reuse everywhere)

These five proofs are the engine of the whole curriculum. Do not re-derive them per topic — **link** to them.

1. **WHY-1 — Place value is a trade, not a digit.** Our system is base-10 positional: each place is 10× the place to its right. 10 ones *are* 1 ten — same amount, different packaging (10 pennies = 1 dime). Every "carry/borrow", every ×/÷10 shift, every decimal rule is this one trade. Source: https://blog.bhanzu.com/parents/what-is-regrouping-in-math ("ten ones snapped into a rod"; "52 was simply renamed as 4 tens and 12 ones, which is still 52"; regrouping "keeps a single digit, 0 to 9, in each place, which is the only thing our number system allows").
2. **WHY-2 — Multiplication is an array / area; division undoes it.** a×b is a rows × b columns, i.e. the area of an a-by-b rectangle. Splitting that rectangle proves the distributive law: a×(b+c) = a×b + a×c (two smaller rectangles side by side have the same total area). Source: https://coprimes.com/math/distributive-property-multiplication/ ("splitting the rectangle into a width of 5 and a width of 3… the sum of the two smaller areas exactly equals the original total area"). Division asks the inverse question: *how many groups of this size fit?* or *how big is each share?*
3. **WHY-3 — A fraction is a number, and ×n/n = ×1.** Multiplying top and bottom by the same n is multiplying by n/n = 1, so the value cannot change; visually you cut every slice into n smaller slices, so you have n× as many slices *and* n× as many shaded slices — the shaded amount is identical. Source: https://www.meaningfulmathco.com/mm_math_terms/equivalent-fractions/ ("multiplying both the numerator and denominator by the same number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by a form of 1"); https://thirdspacelearning.com/us/blog/what-are-equivalent-fractions/.
4. **WHY-4 — Area counts unit squares; volume counts unit cubes; V = base area × height.** A rectangle a×b holds a rows of b unit squares, so area = a×b. Cut-and-slide turns a parallelogram into a rectangle (same base, same height, same area); two copies of a triangle make a parallelogram (hence ½). A prism is layers of its base stacked h high, so volume = (area of base) × height. Source: https://www.mathteacherzhang.com/lessons/003-what-is-area/; https://classmathematics.com.au/resources/wa/year-9/maths/measurement/volume-of-prisms/.
5. **WHY-5 — "Do the same to both sides" is the only equation rule.** Cross-multiplication, solving x, balancing scales: multiplying/dividing both sides of an equality by the same non-zero number leaves it equal. Cross-multiplication is just multiplying a/b = c/d by bd so the denominators cancel (ad = bc). Source: https://math.tools/blog/why-does-cross-multiplication-work ("multiplying both sides by b, then by d… On the left, b cancels. On the right, d cancels").

| 6.12 | **比例 — 意义/基本性质/正比例/反比例/比例尺/图形放大缩小 (G6下)** | A proportion is an *equation of two equal ratios*. 正比例: y = kx (ratio constant). 反比例: xy = k (product constant). 比例尺 is a ratio of map to real. | 6.4, 6.6 | **Proof:** Cross-multiplication (WHY-5): a/b = c/d ⟺ ad = bc — multiply both sides by bd and the denominators cancel (https://math.tools/blog/why-does-cross-multiplication-work). 正比例 works because the *ratio* y/x is the same at every point — the graph is a straight line through the origin. 反比例 works because the *product* xy is the same — if x doubles, y must halve to keep the product. 比例尺 works because it is a ratio: 1:10000 means 1cm on the map *is* 10000cm in reality (the map is a scale model). | Cross-multiplying when *adding* fractions (there is no equation, so there is nothing to multiply — see WHY-5 source) / "if x goes up, y always goes up" (true for 正比例, false for 反比例) / reading a 1:10000 map as "1cm = 10000 *metres*". | 6.13 | 鸽巢问题 (G6下 数学广角; B: G6上 六 复习与关联) | If n+1 pigeons go into n holes, one hole has ≥2 pigeons. | 2.8, 2.11 | **Proof:** Put one pigeon in each hole first — that uses n pigeons. | 6.14 | 综合与实践 / 整理和复习 (G6下; all grades) | Revision is *re-connecting* topics: the same idea (place value, bar model, ×1) appears everywhere. | All | The spiral works *because* each topic is the same few ideas (WHY-1 to WHY-5) in new clothes — recognising the old idea in the new topic is what "understanding" means. | Treating each unit as a separate list of steps to memorise. Wrong/missing because the steps are forgotten; the *ideas* transfer. | Concept map linking each topic to its WHY-proof; mixed-problem sets that require choosing the model. | *"This problem is a bar model again — I just need to find the whole and the parts."* |

**The "why the misconception is wrong" pattern to reuse in the app:** for every misconception, the app should (a) *show* the error with the visual (e.g. the 2/5 bar is shorter than the 1/2 bar, so 1/2+1/3 cannot be 2/5), (b) name the *over-generalised true rule* behind it (whole-number rules applied to decimals; addition rules applied to multiplication), and (c) ask the child to explain it back (§2 Explain-back column). Research basis: misconceptions are prior knowledge misapplied and must be *exposed and resolved through conflict discussion* (Bell/Swan, via https://www.scribd.com/document/274675579/MathNavigator-MisconceptionsError — "without exposure of pupils misconceptions and their resolution through conflict discussion, students may not know why a mistake occurred").

## 5. Abstraction cliffs per grade — where students get stuck, and the visual that best helps

| Grade | Cliff (the moment understanding most often breaks) | Why it is a cliff | Best visual / interactive rescue (and the WHY it carries) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | **10 as a bundle (1.6–1.7) and 凑十/破十 (1.9/1.11).** The child can count to 20 but does not see 13 as 10+3. | First *re-packaging* of a number: the same quantity, two descriptions. Without it, all later place value is rote. | **Ten-frame + bundle-10-sticks animation.** The frame *fills* — the child sees 10 become one thing. WHY-1. |
| **G2** | **Regrouping (2.3) and multiplication as an array (2.5), then division as its inverse (2.8).** "Carry the 1" as a magic digit; × as a chant; ÷ as a different subject from ×. | First time a written algorithm *hides* the quantities (the carried 1 is a ten, not a 1); first time an operation is defined by another operation. | **Base-ten trade animation (2.3) + array that rotates (2.5) + dealing/sharing animation (2.8).** Never column-first; never chant-first. |
| **G3** | **Fractions as numbers (3.7) and two-digit × two-digit (3.11).** "1/4 > 1/3 because 4>3"; "23×15 = 23×5 + 23×1". | First number that is not a count (a *relationship* between two numbers); first multiplication where the partial products must be *placed* by value. | **Fraction bars on one whole + number line (3.7); 4-box area model colour-matched to the column method (3.11).** |
| **G4** | **Decimals as fractions (4.11) and the distributive law as a *tool* (4.10).** "0.50 > 0.5"; "×10 means add a 0"; laws as tricks. | Whole-number rules that were *true for years* now fail; the child must *unlearn by generalising* (the rule was always "digits shift", not "add a 0"). | **Sliding-digits place-value chart (4.11) + split-rectangle for the distributive law (4.10).** Show the old rule *breaking* on 2.4×10, then show the deeper rule working. |
| **G5** | **Decimal ×/÷ (5.1/5.3) and fraction +/− with different denominators (5.11).** "Multiplication makes bigger"; "1/2+1/3=2/5". | The two most durable false generalisations in primary maths meet the two operations where they fail hardest. | **Unit-square overlap area model (5.1) + re-slicing animation (5.11).** Both are *the same* idea (make the pieces the same size, then count) — teach them as one idea. |
| **G6** | **Fraction division (6.2), negative × negative (6.9), and proportion vs additive thinking (6.12).** "Invert and multiply — why?"; "−×− = −"; "if x goes up, y goes up". | First operation whose *procedure* looks arbitrary; first sign rule that contradicts intuition; first relationship that is *multiplicative* where the child expects additive. | **How-many-fit bars → make-the-divisor-1 (6.2); pattern table + flip animation (6.9); ratio table / double number line (6.12).** Each cliff gets *two* independent proofs (§3, Math Doctors technique). |

**Cross-grade pattern:** every cliff is a place where a *true rule from an earlier grade is over-generalised* (counting → fractions; whole numbers → decimals; addition → multiplication; "bigger" → negatives). The rescue is always the same: go back to the concrete/pictorial version, *show* the old rule breaking, and show the deeper rule (WHY-1 to WHY-5) working. That is CPA as a *repair* loop, not just a teaching sequence (Singapore Math source: "CPA is not three separate lessons… A strong teacher loops back to concrete materials the moment a child stumbles" — https://bhanzu.com/parents/what-is-singapore-math).

## 6. Recommended app content outline — grade → unit → concept → lesson (with interaction type)

(2) **One visual language:** number bond → bar model → percent/ratio bar → double number line, carried across all six grades. (3) **Edition switch:** each unit is tagged A (pre-2024), B (2024–26 revised), or Both; the app ships both orders (§1) and defaults to the edition the school selects. (4) Interaction types are given per concept; "—" means the concept is a *fact/definition* best taught by a short animation + check, not a manipulative. Lesson counts are *recommended* (1 lesson ≈ one focused session); total ≈ 340 lessons across G1–G6, which is the honest scale of "ALL knowledge" — do not compress it into a summary course.
